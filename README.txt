FLIGHT CHECKLISTS – OFFLINE SETUP (about 10 minutes, once)

1. Unzip this file in the Files app (tap it once).
2. Sign in or sign up at github.com in Safari.
3. Tap + > New repository. Give it a name (e.g. checklists), set it to Public, create it.
4. Tap "uploading an existing file" and add every file from the unzipped folder:
   index.html, airports.js, runways.js, sw.js, manifest.webmanifest, manifest-classic.webmanifest,
   icon-180.png, icon-192.png, icon-512.png, icon-classic-180.png, icon-classic-192.png, icon-classic-512.png,
   and the lib folder (pdf.js and pdf.worker.js, used to read the LIDO package).
   Tap Commit changes.
5. In the repository: Settings > Pages > Source: Deploy from a branch > Branch: main, folder / (root) > Save.
6. After a minute or two the page shows your address, e.g. https://yourname.github.io/checklists/
7. Open that address in Safari > Share > Add to Home Screen (keep "Open as Web App" on).
8. Open it once from the Home Screen while online. From then on it works offline.

THE BAR AT THE BOTTOM: Checklist, Timers (countdowns and crew rest on one page), Briefing, Airports and More
(event log, Layover, flight log, checklists, untick all, new flight, search, settings). Away from Timers, its tab shows the time left to the
next countdown, or to your rest. The bar steps aside while you edit or type.

EVENT LOG in More: one tap stamps the time and the position (the phone's GPS, which works in airplane mode on an
iPhone, or the flight plan's position once the LIDO package is loaded); copy or share it.

PREVIOUS FLIGHTS (your arrivals and departures, like the "Previous flights" in a Notes page): on an airport's page in
Airports, under its notes, tap Log arrival or Log departure. The flight number and the date fill in for the flight
that's set (from the package or Layover); write what to remember, one point per line (STAR, approach, runway, taxi,
stand, clearance…), and Save. The chips under the box start a line for you, with the runway in use when the package
has it. Photos can go with it. The newest come first; tap one to change or delete it. Tapping Log arrival again for
the same flight opens the one you wrote. At a glance shows the last arrival and departure there.
More > Flight log lists all of them, newest first, with a Find box, and + adds a past one for any airport.
A Flight log block in a checklist has the same buttons for the current flight; a Last time here block shows your last
arrival and departure at the destination (or departure). The old flight log was moved over: each flight became a
departure and an arrival with its stand, taxi, runway, route and notes as lines; taxi times and registrations went.

THE TAB BAR has six places: Checklist, Timers, Briefing, Airports, Layover and More. Choose your own in
More > Edit the tab bar (or hold the bar, or More > Settings > Tab bar): up to five, More always last; tap one to take it
out or put it in, the arrow moves it left. Flight log and Event log can be tabs too. What isn't in the bar is in More.
Each device keeps its own choice. The app also opens where you left off: the page you had open (and the trip on
Layover) when it was closed less than 12 hours ago.

THE APP ICON: More > Settings > App icon: Contrail (the new one: a plane drawing the check at sunset) or Checklist (the original).
  Each device keeps its own. The Home Screen keeps the icon the app was added with, so to change it there add the app
  again: in Safari (Settings gives you the link, ending ?icon=classic or ?icon=contrail), Share > Add to Home Screen.
  The new one has its own storage: bring your things over with Online backup, or Back up and Restore, then remove
  the old one.

LAYOVER (its own tab, with the flag of where you are or go next; it was called Prepare): your next trips, one page
each. The tab opens the trip you're on, or the next one; the trip you're on stays in the list until you fly out.
On a wide iPad the trips sit on the left, each with the country's flag.
- The layover is the rest: from the end of duty (landing + 30 minutes) to the report for the flight out, shown as
  hours:minutes (e.g. 24:25). Without a report time for that flight it counts to the departure and says so.
- At the top: a photo of the city (Wikipedia's picture for the city, fetched once when online and kept for offline;
  without one, the colour of the sky there now), the city, the local time there and how far it is from Dubai, and a
  countdown to report.
- Times: leaving Dubai (report, off blocks), landing (local, Dubai time and UTC, daylight or dark), the layover, and
  leaving again. Wake-up and Pick-up are set by hand for each flight out, in local time; the app remembers how long
  before departure they were at that airport and offers it next time. Each can add a countdown to Timers.
- The timeline: the place and Dubai side by side, from landing to departure. Drag the red line (iPad) or slide the
  strip (iPhone): "Sat 12:25 in New York = Sat 21:25 in Dubai", with home awake or asleep and the weather then. Dark is
  night at the place, and the hours home is asleep (22-07). Tap the purple lane, or Add, to put in sleep, a nap, a
  workout, a meal, a call home or anything else, with a note; tap one to change or delete it. Wake-up and pick-up show
  on it too. On top, the weather hour by hour (Open-Meteo): temperature and sky, rain shaded blue, and good hours to
  be outside outlined green (daylight, dry, 8-28 degrees, not while you sleep), listed under it with the rain.
- Weather for your stay (Open-Meteo, with the UV), money (the rate to AED from a free daily source, a converter both
  ways; kept for offline), emergency
  numbers (built in for the countries Emirates flies to, tap to call) and your own numbers for that airport (hotel,
  station, embassy), good to know (plugs, volts, driving side, clock, money, tipping, tap water, shop hours, air
  quality during the stay, public holidays while you're there, and a few phrases the iPhone reads out where English
  isn't spoken), your Layover notes and last time there. Below: all the notes for its airports, and live METAR/TAF.
  Under the weather come the hotel and your places, then getting around and the rest; the emergency numbers are the
  last card on the page.
- On the iPhone the page is more compact (about a quarter shorter; the iPad stays as it was): the photo and the top
  of the timeline keep their size, everything else sits closer. The report countdown is in the Leave Dubai line; each
  plan is one line with the Dubai time short (DXB 15:00-23:30); the time in the weather title updates the forecast;
  Apple | Google is in the Getting around title; airport, ferries and taxis are one line each (tap one for the rest);
  the places by public transport and Near the hotel are one row that slides; "Add a tip" opens the tip field. The
  timeline hint and the note about the mid-market rate only show on the iPad (the rate's date still shows when old).
- Getting around: built in for about 100 airports crews stay at (checked November 2026): how to pay (tap your bank
  card, Suica in Apple Wallet...), the airport train, ferries or night trains, taxis and ride apps. Your places and
  "Somewhere else" open public transport directions from the hotel. Add your own tips. Elsewhere it links to Wikivoyage.
  "Maps open in" picks Apple Maps or Google Maps for every map button on the page (the hotel, near the hotel, Go);
  each device remembers its own choice. Google's links open the Google Maps app when it's installed, else Safari.
- Hotel: a city can have several hotels, each with its address, Wi-Fi (Copy for the password), breakfast, gym, pool,
  check-out and notes, and the flight numbers that stay there (EK414, EK412). A trip uses the hotel of its flight
  number; Change picks another one for this trip only, or for every trip on that flight. With several hotels and none
  for the flight, it asks once and remembers. The room number is kept for the trip. Near the hotel opens Maps for a
  supermarket, pharmacy, coffee, gym, ATM or laundry. (The old single hotel note became the first hotel.)
- Your places: type a name and Add: the app searches OpenStreetMap and lists the matches nearest the hotel first, so
  you pick the right branch of a chain; or paste a place shared from Apple Maps or Google Maps (Share > Copy); or keep
  just the name (offline it's kept by name to pin later). A pinned place has its exact spot and address, a type from
  the map (cafe, restaurant, shop...) and its distance from the hotel; Maps opens that place in Apple Maps or Google
  Maps (the exact one: from its link or its pin), and Go is there if you want to go. The places under Getting around
  still give public transport directions from the hotel. Tap a place to change its name, type or note, or find it again.
  A map above the list shows the hotel (H) and the pinned places, numbered as in the list: drag, pinch, double-tap
  or + and - to zoom, the square to see everything, tap a pin for Go. Pin the hotel from its card. The map is
  OpenStreetMap; the parts you've looked at are kept, so it works offline on the layover.
  The search keeps to about 60 km around the hotel (or the airport, for the hotel itself). When nothing has that exact
  name there, it tries again without small words and the city's name, then word by word (for a hotel, among hotels
  only, the names with the most of your words first), then the address you saved; only then anywhere, under "Further
  away". OpenStreetMap sometimes knows a hotel by an older name, or has the building without one. Still not there?
  Pick on the map: move the map until the pin sits on the place, then Use this spot.
  Links from Maps: a long link (with the spot in it) pins the place straight away, also pasted into Add. The short
  links the iPhone shares now (maps.apple/p/..., maps.app.goo.gl/...) don't say where the place is, so the app keeps
  the link and asks you to pick the place from the list or on the map; then it has its distance and its pin. Just
  the link saves it unpinned (Maps then opens the link).
  Airport positions are now to about 1 km, so distances from the airport are right.
  Before you go has left the Layover page: make it a checklist of your own among the checklists.
- To buy here: a shopping list per city that stays until you tick it, on every trip there; the trip list shows "2 to buy".
Holidays come from Nager.Date, or are worked out by the date-holidays library (jsDelivr) when that doesn't answer;
air quality from Open-Meteo. Both are fetched when online and kept.
Import roster reads the roster report from the crew portal ("Emirates Crew Roster (Detailed Report)", the PDF or its
text), or CrewPulse's event notes. Only the flights are read (no names, days off, standby or training); times are
local at each airport. It shows what's new, changed or the same before adding; a changed flight keeps your wake-up,
pick-up and plan.
With sync on (More → Settings), Layover syncs like the notes: trips, wake-up and pick-up, what you planned on the
timeline, hotels (with Wi-Fi, flight numbers and their pin), the hotel and room picked for a trip,
places, tips, the shopping lists, numbers, removed duties and the list for new trips. The same item added on both
devices before they synced shows once. The event log stays on each device. The sync file is now
format 4 (it carries the hotels and shopping lists): a device still on an older version says it can't read it until you
tap Update there, so it can't drop what it doesn't know.

THE FLIGHT STRIP under the clock appears once Set flight has an ETD and an ETA: a plane at the time now, the time
flown, the time to go, and your break from Crew rest in purple with a countdown ("rest in 2:40", then "1:10 left").
Tap the strip to change the flight times, or the rest label to open Crew rest. It goes 3 hours after the ETA.

Your checklists are stored on the iPad, not on GitHub. The files on GitHub are only the empty app.
Deleting the Home Screen icon deletes its data, so use More > Settings > Back up now and then.

UPDATING LATER: in the repository tap Add file > Upload files, upload the new files over the old ones and commit.
The installed app downloads the new version in the background while it's open and online, then shows
an Update button at the top left. Tap it to switch now; otherwise the new version is used the next time the app starts.
It looks for a new version at most every 10 to 15 minutes, and never while the iPad is offline, so airplane mode
doesn't bring up the iPadOS "Turn Off Airplane Mode" alert.

ONLINE BACKUP (optional): More > Settings > Online backup copies everything to a PRIVATE GitHub repository
while the app is open and online. You need a private repository and a fine-grained access token
limited to that repository with Contents: Read and write. The steps are shown in the app.

SYNC BETWEEN iPAD AND iPHONE (optional, needs Online backup): on the iPad, More > Settings > Online backup and sync >
turn on "Sync with your other devices". On the iPhone, open the same address in Safari > Share > Add to Home Screen,
open it, then More > Settings > Online backup and sync, enter the same repository and token and choose
"Use the synced checklists". From then on checklists, airport notes, previous flights, Layover (trips, wake-up and
pick-up, timeline plans, hotels, places and their pins, numbers), countdown and crew rest set-up and photos stay the same on both, while the app is open and online: a change is sent a few seconds after you make it,
and the other device checks every 20 seconds and whenever it is opened. Changes made offline are sent when you are
back online. Edits to different items on both devices are combined; if the same item was changed on both, the later
change wins. Ticks, answers, filled-in fields, times, reminders, folding and the event log stay on each device.

PHOTOS: each photo is kept on the device in three sizes: a small one for the lists, a 2048-pixel copy that the
viewer opens, and the original (up to 8 megapixels), which the viewer loads only when you zoom in past the copy. Big
originals on their own could come up black or blurry on the iPad when it was short of memory (Slide Over, or heavy
apps open in flight). If the original still can't open, the copy stays on screen with a note, never a black screen.
Photos added before version 99 get their copy the first time they're opened, or in the background while the app is
idle. Nothing needs the internet; a photo that hasn't come over from your other device yet says so in the viewer.

EDITING: tap the pencil at the top. The checklist shows as an outline: one line per section, header and item, with small tags
for notes, links and photos. Tap anything to open it as a page (text, note, type, sub-items, links, photos, reminder, in or out
a level, duplicate, move, delete); Cancel and Done stay at the top. In a new item, Return keeps it and starts the next one. Drag ≡
to move a line, or hold it over an item to put it inside. "+ Add item or header" ends each section. Select picks several items
to move, indent, duplicate, change or delete at once. The round arrow next to Done undoes your edits one step at a time.
On an iPad in full screen the outline stays on the left and the page opens on the right.
NOTEPADS: the height you pick in the notepad's settings (S, M, L, XL) is its size. "+ More space" adds room for the flight
you're on; New flight clears the drawing and takes the notepad back to its size.
QUICK CHANGES: without Edit, press and hold an item for Edit, add a note, a link, a sub-item or an item below, duplicate,
move or delete.
Write a check and its response with dots between them, like "Parking brake .... SET", and the response lines up on the right. Three spaces instead of the dots, like "Fuel   12.4 t", put the rest on the right without dots.

BRIEFING: More > New flight > Load the OFP takes the LIDO package. iPadOS doesn't hand a copied PDF to web apps, so
either make a one-time Shortcut (Shortcuts > + > name it "Copy for Checklists" > details: Show in Share Sheet, receive PDFs >
actions: Base64 Encode with Line Breaks None, then Copy to Clipboard) and use Share > Copy for Checklists in Lido, then touch
and hold the box and Paste; or paste copied text; or drag the PDF in; or open it from Files. It fills in the flight
(From, To, alternates and their diversion times, ETD, scheduled arrival as ETA, call sign, TOD countdown)
and the Briefing tab shows the NOTAMs for departure, destination, alternates and en-route airports, the
SIGMETs on your route, company alerts and the package weather. The PDF is read on the iPad; nothing is sent anywhere.
The NOTAMs that matter for your times and runways come first. The rest are folded, never hidden.
LIVE WEATHER IN THE BRIEFING: with the weather relay set up, the Briefing keeps the package's own airports up to
date (departure, take-off alternate, destination, alternates), whatever flight the app is set to, so a package for a
future flight gets the newest METAR and TAF too. En-route airports update when you open them. The package card says
"Live weather · updated 2210Z" with an Update button (or "Weather from the package" without the relay); every report
says "LIDO package" or "live". The newer of the two is shown. Each card shows your ETD/ETA at that airport, the TAF
lines for it, and says when the TAF ends before (or starts after) your time. This shows only when the package
is the flight set in the app and that time is still ahead; a package you read just to brief shows its weather plainly.
New weather goes into the cards where they are: the page doesn't redraw, so the NOTAM you're reading stays where it is
and a search you're typing keeps the keyboard.
FORECAST CHART IN THE BRIEFING (v117): under the METAR and TAF of the departure, destination, alternates and take-off
alternate (and under From, To and the alternates before a package is loaded) there's a forecast chart like AeroWeather's:
UTC hours, temperature with the dew point under it (amber when they're within 2°), wind arrow and speed with the
gust in red when it's 5 kt or more above the wind, a line graph of wind and gust, the crosswind on your runway
(gusts counted; T = tailwind) or on the best runway for that hour when none is set, rain/snow/fog/thunder with the
chance in %, cloud cover in octas, visibility (∞ = 10 km or more, amber under 8, red under 3) and local time.
Night hours are shaded. "24 h" shows every hour; "84 h" every 3 hours (gust and chance of rain are the most in those
3 hours); your choice is kept. Swipe sideways for later hours.
The forecast is DWD MOSMIX (Deutscher Wetterdienst), read from the nearest MOSMIX station within 30 km through Bright
Sky (api.brightsky.dev, free, no key); the footer names the station and how far away it is. Where there's no station
it uses Open-Meteo's model forecast and says so. Online, it loads a fresh one when the saved one is over an hour old;
the last one stays on the device (12 airports) and keeps working offline, e.g. in flight: the columns start from the
time now, up to about 3½ days after it was loaded, and the footer says when. Update on the package card loads it again. It needs no weather relay. Not in it yet: the cloud ceiling (Bright Sky doesn't give it),
so there's no ceiling row or VFR/IFR bar.
NOTAMs follow the same rule: for your flight (until 2 h after its ETA) they're read against your ETD/ETA, the ones
not active then dimmed as "Not at your time"; for a package you read just to brief, or a flight that's over, every
NOTAM is shown at full strength, rated on its content only, with "From …" when it starts later than now.
OLD WEATHER: a METAR more than 12 h old or a TAF whose time is over is never shown as current weather; the card says
"No recent METAR. The last one here is from 30 Oct 1730Z (17 days old)" with Update. Airports (full screen) updates
the weather of the airport you open. Ages past two days are given in days.
IPAD FULL SCREEN (the side list and the page beside it scroll separately): when the app has the whole screen (800 points wide or more), the Briefing shows the flight's
airports down the side (departure, take-off alternate, destination, each alternate, en route) with NOTAM counts,
weather category and keyword dots; tap one to see its runway, METAR and TAF side by side, and its NOTAMs one under
another with their full text (nothing to tap open). The weather stays at the top while you scroll the NOTAMs;
Fold shrinks it to one line (the METAR), tap that line to open it again. Airports shows your airports down the side (this flight, its alternates,
all airports), the notes in the middle, and At a glance on the right: local time now and at ETD/ETA, daylight,
runway in use, weather, NOTAMs here (tap to open them in the Briefing) and Last time here. In Slide Over, Split View
or on the iPhone both pages stay as before.
RUNWAY IN USE: the departure and arrival runways come from the OFP. When one changes, tap the runway row at the top
of the departure or destination tab and pick the new one (the runways in that airport's NOTAMs, or type one). The NOTAMs
for your runway, the runway highlights and the summary follow it; the row turns amber and shows the OFP's, with Back to
the OFP runway. Each alternate has its own runway row too (on its page on the iPad, under its name on the iPhone):
the OFP's, or tap it to pick another; its NOTAMs, highlights and wind follow it, and the side list says "(changed)".
Loading an updated copy of the same package keeps your choices unless its OFP has a different runway.
Pasting only NOTAMs or weather later (the Briefing's load button) updates the briefing for those airports.
Tap the bookmark on a NOTAM to keep it. Kept NOTAMs show in a NOTAMs block you add to any checklist section
(Edit > NOTAMs), for the airports you choose: departure, destination, alternates, en route, company alerts.
KEPT ONLY: the Kept chip first in the row over the NOTAMs shows only the NOTAMs you kept (bookmarked), on every
airport until you tap it again or Show all; it turns off by itself when you un-keep the last one.
NOTAM KEYWORDS: your own words (22L, ILS, CRANE…), each in its own colour. NOTAMs with a Highlight word come
first with the word marked; tap a word's chip to see only those NOTAMs. NOTAMs with a Hide word (BIRD…) fold away
at the end of each list, one tap to show them; kept NOTAMs are never hidden. Whole words only: 22L finds
RWY 04R/22L. Change the list with the gear next to the chips, or press and hold any word in a NOTAM to highlight
it everywhere, at that airport only, or hide NOTAMs with it. Option in the list: Checkup summary, a card at the
top of the Briefing with your words at each airport of the flight. Keywords are kept with your settings (and backup).
SEARCH EVERY NOTAM: on the full iPad screen, the box at the top of the side list; on the iPhone and in Slide Over, the
magnifier at the top of the Briefing. It looks through every NOTAM in the package (and their numbers), for words that
start with what you type: CRANE finds CRANES, RANE doesn't. Several words must all be there (CRANE 12L). The results
are grouped by airport in the package's order with the words marked; in the side list the airports without any fade.
Open goes to that airport with the search cleared. Up to 200 results; add a word to narrow it down.
WHAT CHANGED: load a newer copy of the same flight's package and a sheet shows what's different from the copy you
had: ETD and ETA, runways, alternates, aircraft, fuel, trip time, levels, route; new NOTAMs; NOTAMs no longer in the
package (only for airports the new copy has); new TAFs. Tap a new NOTAM to go to its airport. In the Briefing the package card
says "New copy 1312Z · 4 new · 2 removed · ETD +35 min" with See changes; airports show "+2 new"; new NOTAMs are
marked New and come first; the ones gone are struck through at the end of their airport (folded when more than 3).
The marks stay until Clear the marks in the sheet, or until you load another copy. Loading only NOTAMs does the same
for those airports.
CREW BRIEF: Briefing > the package card > Crew brief. The flight on one page, made from the package and the live
weather: ETD, ETA, trip, fuel and levels; departure and destination with the runway, the wind on it, the METAR, the
TAF lines at your time, the worst TAF wind for your runway and the trend; each alternate with its runway, the wind and
the TAF at its ETA; SIGMETs on your route while you're in them; your kept NOTAMs under their airport; and what changed
since the last copy. Times are the planned ones, also for a package you only brief. Print or PDF prints just the
page, light, on one A4 for a normal flight (in the print sheet, Share > Save to Files keeps a PDF). Send shares it as
text (Messages, AirDrop, WhatsApp) or copies it. It's a summary; the OFP, NOTAMs and OM stay the reference.
WIND ON YOUR RUNWAY: the runway row always shows the wind on it in a few letters (HW 7 XW 11R, tailwind as TW), from
the METAR. Tap Wind on a weather card for the rest: headwind or tailwind and crosswind with gusts, a small compass, and what a varying wind (250V320) can do. "Around your ETA" gives the strongest
wind the TAF has at your time and its components. All runways now lists every runway, most into the wind first, with
yours marked. The runway is the OFP's or the one you set, alternates included. Tailwind and crosswinds of
15 kt or more are amber. True runway headings come from
runways.js (OurAirports, public domain); for an airport it doesn't have, the runway number is used (within a few
degrees). Wind and Trend are one setting for every card; tap again to hide.
WEATHER TREND (off until you tap Trend): the last METARs of up to 3 hours, as a table on the iPad, as small bars on
the iPhone, with worse values in amber and a line saying what changed: visibility, ceiling, new weather and since
when, a small temperature spread, QNH, wind. The app keeps every METAR it gets (6 hours, on the device). The relay
from version 3 sends the last 3 hours at once; with an older relay the trend fills in as the weather updates.
On the full iPad screen, Wind and Trend sit under the weather that stays at the top (also when it's folded).

LIVE WEATHER (optional): the Briefing tab can also show live METAR, TAF (aviationweather.gov) and US D-ATIS (atis.info)
through a small free Cloudflare Worker you create once. Paste weather-relay-worker.js into a new "Hello World" Worker,
deploy it, and enter its address (https://….workers.dev) in Briefing.
UPDATING THE RELAY (version 3, for the 3-hour trend): Cloudflare dashboard > Workers & Pages > your relay > Edit code,
select all, paste the new weather-relay-worker.js, Deploy. The address stays the same; nothing to change in the app.
